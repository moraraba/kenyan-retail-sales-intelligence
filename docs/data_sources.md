## Why the sales data is synthetic, and how it was made realistic
Real retailer till data is confidential; the brief permits a generated dataset. The generator is deterministic and
documented line by line in `src/generate_data.py`. Realism comes from:

* **Real context data drives the simulation** - monthly prices follow actual KNBS inflation (food at 1.25x the headline
  rate, plus the 2026 fuel/food jump), and demand reacts to the actual gazetted holidays.
* **Kenyan shopping behaviour** - month-end payday window (25th-3rd), Saturday peaks, December festive peak, January
  slump, school-term starts (Jan, late Apr/May, late Aug) for stationery, M-Pesa as the dominant and growing payment
  method.
* **Customer lifecycle** - members with different visit rates, gradual "drift" before they stop, and churn hazards that
  differ by segment.
* **Planted, documented business events** - a competitor near the Nakuru stores from Feb 2026; a cooking-oil promo that
  works and a personal-care promo that doesn't; product affinities (tea+sugar, unga+sukuma...). The planted truth is
  saved in `data/raw/_ground_truth_customers.csv` and is used **only** to sanity-check findings, never as model input.
* **Realistic dirt** - duplicates, test receipts, mixed date formats, 15 spellings of payment methods, `KES 1,234.00`
  strings, missing prices, keying-error quantities, impossible birth years, inconsistent county casing.

## Limitations of the data
* Absolute volumes represent a sample of tills (about 225 receipts per store per day on average would be a real
  supermarket; this data has ~22) - ratios, shares, trends and model behaviour are meaningful, absolute shilling totals
  are illustrative.
* County population is from 2019; growth since then is not modelled.
* The CPI index is reconstructed from 12-month inflation rates (monthly growth = (1+yoy)^(1/12)-1) - an approximation
  of the official index level, adequate for deflating revenue.
